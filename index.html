Abortion is too hard to find from the Gorge.

The clinics that refer stay silent in search. The centers against abortion say the word on every page. Fixing that is free, and the money for more is here.

abortion providers in Skamania, Klickitat, Hood River or Wasco county. None, on either side of the river. Even clinicians here are surprised by it.

In one minute

  1. There is no abortion provider in the Gorge. The nearest in-person care is in Portland or Yakima, 60 to 150 minutes each way; the other route is telehealth, with pills by mail.
  2. Finding that out is unacceptably hard, because the only organizations that use the word "abortion" on their websites, in a way Google can index, are the ones against it. The clinics that test, counsel and refer do not say the word online. That is the clinics' choice, and theirs to fix.
  3. The numbers: in 36 town searches, an anti-abortion center was the first result in 10 and on page one in 22. A Gorge clinic appeared in 14, and none of those pages said what a pregnant person could get there.
  4. The detour hurts. A week lost can push a patient past the window for pills by mail and past the abortion fund's 48-hour notice; a miscarrying patient leaves with an ultrasound and no treatment; elsewhere, women sent home from centers like these with a missed ectopic pregnancy have nearly died.
  5. The fix is a web page. A clinic that says "all-options counseling and abortion referral," "free pregnancy test" and "early pregnancy appointment" in words a search can match, in English and Spanish, is the only Gorge result for that search the day the page goes up.
  6. The money is here. Washington's first-year rural health award ($181M) is spent, and Skyline Health is on the maternal-services list. Year two ($177M) is budgeted by the end of December. Oregon ($197M a year) already funded One Community Health and Providence Hood River.

WHAT A RESIDENT FINDS

Type "abortion" and your town. Here is who answers.

On October 28, 2026, Gorge Access ran 42 Google searches a Gorge resident might type, in a clean browser with no account and no history. For six towns and six phrases, this grid shows what kind of site came up first. The anti-abortion centers rank because they built a page for every town, in English and Spanish, with the words people type.

The directory gets it wrong by accident, too. 211info's entries for One Community Health's Hood River clinic and its high-school clinic say the program "offers … abortions." The service tag on the same entries says "Abortion Referrals." A patient reads the description, not the tag.

And a rule Gorge Access holds itself to: a provider page that doesn't appear, or doesn't mention a service, is a visibility finding, not proof the service isn't offered. That is exactly the problem. Patients can't tell either.

WHAT THE DETOUR COSTS

A week, and sometimes the option itself.

The centers' pages ask a patient to book a consultation and get an ultrasound first. Their posted hours set the pace. A telehealth prescription, the option most Gorge residents would actually use, is a same-day intake and pills in one to three business days.

Four false claims on the centers' own pages

These are quoted so the record is exact. Each one is false, and each is followed by what the evidence shows.

False claimWhile you can order abortion pills by mail, it is a risk to your health and safety.

Hope Medical Clinic's abortion-pill page.

The evidence: the FDA's own program has not required an in-person visit since January 2023 and allows certified pharmacies to mail the medication. A study of 6,034 telehealth medication abortions found 99.8% free of any serious adverse event (Upadhyay et al., Nature Medicine, 2024).

False claimReversal is possible if action is taken after the first dose.

Hope's page, routing patients to Heartbeat International's "Abortion Pill Reversal" hotline.

The evidence: ACOG: such claims "are not based on science and do not meet clinical standards." The only controlled trial was stopped after three of twelve participants hemorrhaged (Creinin et al., 2020).

False claimI'm planning to take the abortion pill. Do I still need an ultrasound? Yes.

Goldendale Pregnancy Resource Center's abortion-pill page.

The evidence: the American Academy of Family Physicians, summarizing ACOG's practice bulletin: "Routine use of ultrasonography before a first-trimester abortion is not necessary" (AFP, 2022). A study of 3,779 medication abortions screened by history alone found the approach "effective and safe" (Upadhyay et al., JAMA Internal Medicine, 2022). The free ultrasound is the visit's hook; on Oct. 28, Hope's site said its medical services were paused.

False claimIf the pregnancy is not viable (no heartbeat) … there is no reason to take the pill.

Goldendale's page.

The evidence: a pregnancy with no heartbeat is an early pregnancy loss, and mifepristone with misoprostol is a standard treatment for it (Schreiber et al., New England Journal of Medicine, 2018). The advice sends a miscarrying patient away from care, from a center that cannot provide it.

SO WHAT

The search result is not the harm. What follows it is.

The clock runs out on the easiest option

Medication abortion by telehealth is available to about 10 or 11 weeks with most providers (70 days under the FDA label). A week lost to a consultation, an ultrasound and a callback can cross that line, and a same-day intake with pills by mail becomes an in-person procedure in Portland or Yakima: the drive, a day off, childcare, more money. Hope's page prices the pill at "$400-$800"; OHSU lists $467. For some patients the detour changes which options remain.

The funding window closes

The Northwest Abortion Access Fund "is not able to accommodate requests that come in 48 hours or less before an appointment." A patient who finally books after the detour may book too late for help paying.

A miscarrying patient is sent away from treatment

The centers offer an ultrasound but no care. Goldendale's page says that with no heartbeat "there is no reason to take the pill." Mifepristone with misoprostol is a standard treatment for early pregnancy loss (Schreiber et al., 2018). She leaves with a diagnosis, no treatment, and advice against the medication that is the treatment.

Emergency contraception gets skipped

Hope's page says Plan B "may prevent the fertilized egg from implanting," which the FDA's 2022 label rejects, and that it may not work above 165 pounds, which the label does not say. Emergency contraception is time-limited, days not weeks. A patient who believes the page may not take it, or may not know the county clinics hand it out same-day.

The misinformation travels with her

She has now read, on a page that calls itself "evidence-based," that pills by mail are a risk to her safety and that an abortion can be "reversed," a protocol whose only controlled trial was stopped after three of twelve women hemorrhaged. Some patients will act on what they read. Studies of these centers' websites and counseling have found the same pattern nationally: inaccurate claims about abortion's risks (Bryant & Levi, 2012; Swartzendruber et al., 2018) and ultrasounds that misstate how far along a pregnancy is (Montoya et al., 2022).

The people with the least slack pay the most

Goldendale and Stevenson residents already make a 60-to-150-minute trip for any in-person care, and Washington residents face a bridge that will close during construction. The detour is added on top. For a teenager, a Spanish speaker routed to Bend, or someone without a car, it may be the end of the search.

It has already gone wrong elsewhere

The Gorge centers' pages do not promise to rule out an ectopic pregnancy; they offer the ultrasound as the first step. In other states, that first step has been the last one before an emergency.

If I trusted those people instead of getting a second opinion, I could have died.

Codyone Cameron, Wisconsin, 2026. Pregnant and in pain, she went to Life's Connection near Milwaukee, an affiliate of Heartbeat International, for a free ultrasound, and told staff she had had an ectopic pregnancy before. She was told this one was normal. Ten days later a clinic found it was ectopic; emergency surgery took both fallopian tubes. The Center for Reproductive Rights has asked Wisconsin's attorney general to investigate centers statewide (Center for Reproductive Rights, Nov. 19, 2026; USA Today).

A Massachusetts woman's paperwork said "viable, in-utero pregnancy." A month later her fallopian tube ruptured.

Clearway Clinic, Worcester, 2023. She found the center by searching online for a nearby ultrasound. A class-action lawsuit alleges the nurse who scanned her was not qualified to diagnose an ectopic pregnancy and that staff followed up urging her to keep the pregnancy (NBC News, June 28, 2023).

It is near impossible to rule out an ectopic.

Anne O'Connor, the lawyer for NIFLA, a national membership organization of these centers, telling them not to advertise that they can. A watchdog found 100 centers in 49 states doing it anyway and asked New York's attorney general to investigate; the diagnosis takes repeated blood tests and often repeated ultrasounds, which the centers cannot provide (NPR, June 24, 2026).

Fewer than 40% of these centers' staff were licensed medical professionals, by the movement's own 2019 count.

Charlotte Lozier Institute figure, as reported by NBC News. The Gorge centers do not name a medical director on their sites. Hope's homepage said on Oct. 28 that its medical services were paused.

It lands on the clinics, too. Patients arrive late, confused about what a clinic offers, or not at all. When no clinic says in writing what it does, people pass around what they have heard, and some of what they have heard is wrong.

The profession's own rules point one way. The AMA tells even an objecting physician to "inform the patient about all relevant options" and refer; ACOG says referral "need not be conceptualized as a repudiation or compromise of one's values." A clinic with no objection that stays silent to avoid a hypothetical complaint is putting a hypothetical offended person ahead of a real patient who is pregnant now, has a time-limited decision, and is about to find an anti-abortion center instead. That is not caution. It is a choice about whose comfort matters, and it is not compassionate care.

THE FREE FIXES

A clinic that says it on its website can be found.

A search engine can only show what a page says. Each of these is one page, written once by whoever maintains a clinic's site. Each one, the day it goes up, is the only Gorge result for that search.

All-options referral, in those words

"We provide pregnancy testing and all-options counseling, and we refer for abortion care." Where the clinic refers, what it does before and after, and how a Washington resident becomes a patient. Title X clinics are already required to offer this counseling at the counter.

Free pregnancy tests

A page that says "free pregnancy test" with the town's name. Today that search in Goldendale or The Dalles returns the centers.

Early pregnancy appointments, before 14 weeks

Dating, options and a plan, while every option is still open. A page that offers that visit, and says so, meets the patient where the centers meet her: at "I just found out."

Emergency contraception and miscarriage care

Each with its own page and the plain phrase in the title. Klickitat County's page ranks first for "emergency contraception goldendale" because it says "available same day."

In Spanish

The centers did it. A Spanish speaker in the Gorge today is routed to them or to Bend.

Name the Washington towns

Google cannot connect "Stevenson," "Bingen" or "White Salmon" to health care unless a page does it.

Fix the listings, and name an owner

Read the 211info, Google Business Profile and WebMD entries as a patient would, correct them, and name one person to check them each quarter. Pages drift.

If one paragraph is all a clinic will post, this is the paragraph, and it works whether or not the clinic provides abortion:

"We do not provide medication or procedural abortion at this site. Our clinicians offer all-options counseling and will refer you. For abortion appointments in Oregon or Washington, use these direct links. Ask our staff for help with insurance, interpretation or travel."

Public agencies here already do this. North Central Public Health District's page says a pregnant client can "talk with the nurse about all pregnancy choices," including abortion. Hood River County's lists "all options counseling & referrals." Washington's and Oregon's health departments publish provider lists and warn, by name, about pregnancy resource centers. The pages have stayed up.

THE FUNDED FIXES

The rural health money is here, and the next budget is being written.

Washington's first-year Rural Health Transformation award is fully committed as of November 5, 2026. Two of its lines reached the Gorge. Year two is $177 million, and the state's budget for it is expected by the end of December.
